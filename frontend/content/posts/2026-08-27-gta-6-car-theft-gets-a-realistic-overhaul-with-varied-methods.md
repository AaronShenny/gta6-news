# GTA 6: Car Theft Gets a Realistic Overhaul with Varied Methods

Grand Theft Auto 6 is set to follow in the footsteps of Red Dead Redemption 2 by significantly increasing its focus on realism. This commitment to detail extends to a core gameplay element: stealing cars. Unlike previous GTA titles where a simple window smash sufficed for any vehicle, GTA 6 will introduce varied methods of car theft, tailored to the type and age of the vehicle.

For **older cars**, players will have two primary options. The traditional method of smashing a window will still be available, albeit likely attracting more attention. Alternatively, a quieter approach involves using a 'slim jim' – a tool familiar to roadside assistance – which will require players to complete a short minigame using their controller's stick. Once inside, these vehicles will need to be hotwired before they can be driven.

**Newer, more technologically advanced vehicles** will present a greater challenge. These cars cannot be accessed via window smashing or slim jims. Instead, players will need to acquire and utilize a 'key cloner.' This device facilitates a 'relay attack,' mimicking the electronic signals of modern key fobs to remotely unlock the car without triggering alarms, a technique mirroring real-world criminal methods. It is presumed that protagonists Jason and Lucia will carry these key cloners as part of their arsenal, adding strategic depth to how players acquire vehicles and navigate Vice City's criminal underworld.

## Key Takeaways
- GTA 6 will feature a heightened focus on realism, extending to its car theft mechanics.
- The method for stealing a car in GTA 6 will depend on whether it's an older or newer model.
- Older cars can be stolen by smashing a window or by using a slim jim, which involves a minigame and subsequent hotwiring.
- Newer cars require a 'key cloner' to perform a 'relay attack,' mimicking key fob signals for silent, remote entry.
- Key cloners are expected to be an inventory item for protagonists Jason and Lucia.
