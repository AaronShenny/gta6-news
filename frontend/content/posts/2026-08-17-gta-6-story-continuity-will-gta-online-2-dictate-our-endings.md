# GTA 6 Story Continuity: Will GTA Online 2 Dictate Our Endings?

A recent discussion on the r/GTA6 subreddit has ignited a debate among fans regarding the potential impact of GTA Online 2 on the single-player story's canonical endings in Grand Theft Auto 6. The user, u/guts1ck, highlighted how Red Dead Redemption 2 featured a strict canonical ending, while Grand Theft Auto V offered multiple choices that some felt were rendered 'pointless' by the subsequent narrative of GTA Online. The core question revolves around whether GTA 6, if it offers multiple story conclusions, will face a similar situation, where an integrated online component could effectively force a single, strict canonical ending, thereby diminishing the player's choice in the single-player narrative. This raises important questions about player agency and narrative consistency in Rockstar's future titles.

## Key Takeaways
- The debate centers on GTA Online 2's potential influence on GTA 6's single-player story endings.
- GTA 5's multiple endings were arguably undermined by GTA Online's established canon.
- Red Dead Redemption 2 featured a single, strict canonical ending.
- Fans are concerned if GTA 6 will force a canonical ending, potentially negating player choices in the narrative.

## FAQ
**What is the main concern about GTA 6's story continuity?**
The primary concern is whether GTA Online 2 will impose a strict canonical ending for GTA 6's single-player story, potentially making player choices in the narrative feel less impactful or ultimately irrelevant.

**How did GTA Online affect GTA 5's multiple endings?**
GTA 5 offered players multiple choices for its ending; however, the ongoing narrative of GTA Online effectively established a canonical outcome, leading some players to feel their chosen single-player endings were invalidated or made 'pointless' in the broader universe.

**What precedent does Red Dead Redemption 2 set regarding story endings?**
Red Dead Redemption 2 featured a single, strict canonical ending, which contrasts with GTA 5's initial approach of offering multiple choices before GTA Online's influence.
