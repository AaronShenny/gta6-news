# Xbox's GTA 6 Streaming Deal: A Game Changer for Market Share and Affordability

Microsoft and Take-Two have announced a significant deal allowing Grand Theft Auto 6 to be streamed directly to Xbox consoles, including the older Xbox One. This strategic move, detailed in a report by Ampere Analysis, is projected to expand GTA 6's addressable market on Xbox by a substantial 30%, reaching an estimated 10 million active Xbox One consoles.

The deal positions Xbox as the most affordable entry point for GTA 6, with Ampere estimating a total cost of just $92 for 50 hours of streaming gameplay, assuming players already own an Xbox One, controller, and screen. This cost is calculated at approximately 0.24 cents per hour. Streaming will utilize an Xbox Series S version of the game, meaning performance may vary based on internet connection quality, and Digital Foundry noted potential frame rate struggles on the Series S.

Crucially, no similar streaming arrangement has been made for PlayStation, leaving an estimated 25 million active PS4 consoles without this option. Ampere Analysis suggests several reasons for this disparity: Take-Two's interest in expanded reach and potentially more favorable financial terms from Microsoft, and Microsoft's robust Azure cloud infrastructure, which offers a superior capacity for handling a large influx of streaming users compared to Sony. Furthermore, Sony's focus appears to be on converting users to its current-gen PS5, rather than enabling GTA 6 sales on older PS4 hardware via streaming.

Players can access GTA 6 streaming via specific cloud playtime packs (e.g., 10 hours for $4, 50 hours for $12) or through Game Pass memberships, which now have instituted hourly streaming limits (e.g., 15 hours for Game Pass Ultimate). Beyond streaming, Xbox also offers a cheaper console entry point, with the Xbox Series S costing $500 compared to the cheapest new PS5 at $600. GTA 6 is set to launch on November 19 for PS5 and Xbox Series X|S.
