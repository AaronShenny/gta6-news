# Take-Two CEO Zelnick: Cloud Streaming to '10x' Gaming Reach, Consoles May Soon Matter Less

Take-Two Interactive CEO Strauss Zelnick recently addressed investors regarding the current state of gaming hardware and its potential impact on the industry, including highly anticipated titles like *Grand Theft Auto 6*. Despite the increased cost of consoles like the PS5 and Xbox Series X|S compared to previous generations, Zelnick expressed confidence that this wouldn't hinder Take-Two's business or *GTA 6* sales, attributing success to delivering compelling experiences. However, he acknowledged that lower hardware prices would be beneficial for wider adoption.

Zelnick highlighted a significant shift towards open systems, noting that PC gaming, which once accounted for a mere 2% of sales, now represents up to 50%. He anticipates this trend will continue. The most significant revelation was Zelnick's strong belief in cloud streaming technology. He projected that within three years, the industry would reach a 'commercial streaming mode' with low latency, potentially multiplying the effective installed base by tenfold. While this won't necessarily lead to a 10x revenue increase, as core gamers already have access, it presents a 'great opportunity' for broadly desirable titles, including *GTA 6*, to reach wider, non-traditional gaming audiences.

The article also notes that console prices are expected to rise again next year, and PC gamers may face challenges with RAM chip production being sold out for 2027, underscoring the relevance of Zelnick's comments on alternative access methods like streaming.

## Key Takeaways
- Take-Two CEO Strauss Zelnick believes rising console costs won't negatively impact GTA 6 sales due to strong game experiences.
- Zelnick prefers lower hardware prices to increase market penetration.
- PC gaming has grown from 2% to 50% of sales and is expected to expand further.
- Take-Two anticipates "commercial streaming mode" with low latency within three years.

- Console prices are projected to increase further, and PC hardware (RAM) faces supply issues for 2027.
