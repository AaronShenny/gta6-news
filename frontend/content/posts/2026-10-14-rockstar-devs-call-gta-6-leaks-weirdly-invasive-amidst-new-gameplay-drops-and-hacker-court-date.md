# Rockstar Devs Call GTA 6 Leaks 'Weirdly Invasive' Amidst New Gameplay Drops and Hacker Court Date

Rockstar Games developers have opened up about the emotional toll of the extensive Grand Theft Auto 6 leaks, with Rockstar North's Aaron Garbut likening the experience to 'somebody’s wading through your dirty washing' and calling it 'weirdly invasive.' These comments, published by Bloomberg, coincide with a new wave of gameplay leaks from an entity known as CyberLeek, who claims this latest drop could be their last. The original hacker responsible for the 2022 breaches has reportedly been apprehended and is set to appear in court on November 9, just days before GTA 6's anticipated release.The security breaches prompted Rockstar to implement a controversial mandatory 'return to office' policy for developers, a measure Rockstar publishing boss Jennifer Kolbe indicates is unlikely to see major changes post-launch, citing the game's high 'steal-ability.'Beyond the leaks, the Bloomberg report unveiled several other intriguing details:*   GTA 6's initial scope was larger, potentially allowing for an earlier release.*   The game features an 'absurd amount of detail,' including in its nightclubs.*   The rumored $1.5 billion development cost is false, though the actual figure remains undisclosed.*   An entire dedicated studio was created solely for generating NPCs.*   Rockstar's realism experiments began with seemingly mundane items like beer and sandwiches.*   Players won't have to endure an eight-year wait for Rockstar's next title after GTA 6.GTA 6 is currently slated for a November 19 release on PS5 and Xbox Series X|S, though the possibility of further delays has been acknowledged.
