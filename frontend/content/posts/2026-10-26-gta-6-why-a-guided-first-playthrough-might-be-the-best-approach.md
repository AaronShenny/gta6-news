# GTA 6: Why a Guided First Playthrough Might Be the Best Approach

A Reddit user challenges the common 'day one' strategy of immediately exploring every corner of a new open-world game like GTA 6. The author suggests that, based on current marketing, GTA 6's story may unfold in chapters, similar to Red Dead Redemption 2, naturally guiding players through different areas of the map. They argue that premature, full-map exploration could diminish the emotional impact and intended narrative reveals, citing a poignant moment in RDR2 where Arthur's first encounter with St. Denis loses its significance if players have already explored the area. The article advocates for trusting the developers' pacing and design for the initial playthrough, allowing the story and environments to make their intended first impression, while reserving extensive, unguided exploration and optimization for subsequent runs. Ultimately, the author acknowledges individual player freedom but encourages consideration for a more structured first experience.

## Key Takeaways
- Avoid immediate, full-map exploration of GTA 6 on day one.
- The game's narrative may feature chapter-based progression, akin to Red Dead Redemption 2.
- Premature exploration can lessen the emotional impact and spoil story-driven reveals.
- Trust the developers to guide the player's first experience of the world and characters.
- Reserve extensive exploration and 'optimal' gameplay strategies for subsequent playthroughs.

## FAQ
**What is the main argument of the article regarding GTA 6 gameplay?**
The article's main argument is that players should resist the urge to immediately explore the entire GTA 6 map on day one, instead advocating for a more guided, story-driven first playthrough to preserve narrative impact.

**Why does the author suggest limiting exploration on the first playthrough?**
The author believes that GTA 6's story might unfold in chapters, similar to RDR2, where new areas are introduced organically. Exploring prematurely could diminish the emotional weight and surprise of these intended narrative reveals.

**Which game does the article use as a primary example?**
Red Dead Redemption 2 (RDR2) is used as the primary example, specifically citing Arthur Morgan's first ride to St. Denis in Chapter 4, to illustrate how prior exploration can devalue a significant narrative moment.

**Does the author completely discourage exploration?**
No, the author suggests exploring the areas the story currently places you in, but advises against venturing into new, story-locked regions until the game naturally guides you there.
