# GTA 6 NPC Behavior: Are Rockstar's Immersive Features Clever Optimization Tricks?

As the highly anticipated release of Grand Theft Auto VI approaches, a programmer has offered a compelling theory regarding the game's non-player character (NPC) behavior. Drawing from an IGN interview that mentioned NPCs reacting to being followed, the author suggests that these seemingly advanced features might be a 'smoke and mirrors' tactic for optimization, rather than a comprehensive overhaul of every NPC's daily life.

The article contrasts this with Red Dead Redemption 2, where NPCs were lauded for their full day-night routines, living out detailed lives from waking up to going home. Given that Leonida, GTA VI's map, is rumored to be three times larger than RDR2's, the programmer argues that replicating full, individualized routines for every NPC would be an impractical and CPU-intensive endeavor. 

Instead, the theory proposes a 'hybrid approach' using 'segmented component based routines.' This means NPCs would have specific sets of behaviors tied to particular areas or venues (e.g., taking selfies at a rooftop bar, ordering drinks, waiters serving tables). These behaviors would be batched and randomized to create a sense of genuine activity and immersion. The 'smoke and mirrors' aspect comes into play when a player attempts to follow an NPC leaving such an area: the NPC might react aggressively or call out the player for stalking, precisely because they don't have a fully programmed 'home' or extended routine to follow. This clever design would allow Rockstar to maintain a high level of immersion without the immense development cost and performance hit of simulating full lives for every single NPC in a vast open world.
