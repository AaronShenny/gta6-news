# Ex-Dishonored Dev Calls GTA Games a "Stab in the Heart" But Still "Amazing"

Raphaël Colantonio, the acclaimed developer behind games like Dishonored and Weird West, offers a nuanced perspective on the Grand Theft Auto series, describing it as both "the biggest tease" and "the biggest disappointment" for fans of immersive sims. While acknowledging Rockstar's unparalleled ability to create "fantastic sandboxes with incredible execution" and "surface simulation," Colantonio expresses a personal disappointment in the series' lack of long-term consequences for player actions. He contrasts this with games like Skyrim, where choices have lasting impacts on the persistent world. Colantonio theorizes that Rockstar intentionally avoids deep consequence systems to prevent player confusion and facilitate high-octane, instanced missions with spectacular set pieces, even if it breaks immersion for him. Despite his personal preferences for deeper reactivity and immersion, he holds "the greatest respect" for Rockstar's achievements and commercial success. He concludes by stating he is "really, really looking forward" to GTA 6, eager to witness how Rockstar will further push their renowned "instant simulation" capabilities. The article also briefly notes GTA 6's November 19 launch, recent leaks, and an upcoming extended trailer on Netflix.

## FAQ
**Who is Raphaël Colantonio?**
Raphaël Colantonio is an industry veteran known for his work on the Dishonored series at Arkane Studios and is currently the head of WolfEye Studios, which developed Weird West.

**Why does Colantonio find GTA games a "stab in the heart"?**
He finds them a "stab in the heart" because, despite their incredible "surface simulation" and execution, they lack long-term consequences for player actions, which he values in immersive sim games.

**What aspect of GTA does Colantonio praise?**
He praises GTA's "fantastic sandbox with incredible execution, incredible cinematography, [and] great gameplay," highlighting Rockstar's mastery of "surface simulation."

**How does Colantonio contrast GTA with games like Skyrim?**
He notes that in Skyrim, player actions have real, lasting consequences in a single instance of the world, whereas GTA's instanced missions reset consequences, which he finds less immersive.

**What are Colantonio's expectations for GTA 6?**
He is "really, really looking forward" to GTA 6 to see how far Rockstar will push their "instant simulation" capabilities, despite his personal preferences regarding player consequences.

**When is GTA 6 launching?**
GTA 6 is scheduled to launch on November 19 for PS5 and Xbox Series X|S.
