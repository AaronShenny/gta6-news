# Beyond Pixels: Why GTA 6's Lucia Isn't a 'Girlfriend Simulator'

This article, an opinion piece from a self-identified female gamer, addresses the controversy surrounding some women's concerns that their boyfriends playing *Grand Theft Auto 6* might be akin to a "girlfriend simulator" due to the prominent female character, Lucia. The author expresses confusion and concern over this viewpoint, asserting that *GTA 6* is not a dating simulator and that fictional characters like Lucia pose no real-world threat or competition to a relationship. Drawing parallels to her own experiences with games like *Baldur's Gate 3* and other media, she argues that fictional relationships and character sexualization are common and should not be equated with real-life infidelity or exploitation. The author encourages women to engage with video games themselves, suggesting that destigmatizing romantic elements in gaming could foster greater female participation. She also dismisses the "cheating" argument, differentiating fictional characters from real individuals exploited in industries like adult entertainment, and points out that sexualization in media is widespread and can apply to male characters from a female gaze perspective.

## Key Takeaways
- GTA 6 is not a 'girlfriend simulator' despite Lucia's importance as a character.
- Fictional video game characters are not real people and do not pose competition or a threat to real-world relationships.
- Concerns about 'cheating' with pixelated characters are unfounded and disproportionate compared to engagement with other forms of media like movies or books.
- Sexualization in games is comparable to that found in movies and TV, and can apply to male characters from a female gaze perspective.
- Encourages women to play video games to better understand the medium and help destigmatize in-game relationships and character interactions.
