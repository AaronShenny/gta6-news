# GTA 6 PC Release: A Historical Look at Rockstar's Console-First Strategy

For dedicated PC gamers eagerly awaiting Grand Theft Auto VI, a look back at the series' release patterns reveals a consistent trend: PC versions typically arrive significantly later than their console counterparts. Historically, only the very first two titles in the franchise, Grand Theft Auto 1 and Grand Theft Auto 2, saw a PC-first or simultaneous release. GTA 1 launched on PC in November 1997 (Europe) and March 1998 (North America), preceding its PS1 release by weeks or months. GTA 2 even had a simultaneous PC and PS1 release in Europe in October 1999, though North America still saw PC first.

However, this trend reversed dramatically from Grand Theft Auto III onwards. GTA III, Vice City, San Andreas, and GTA IV all debuted on PlayStation and Xbox consoles, with PC versions following approximately 7 to 8 months later. The most significant delay was observed with Grand Theft Auto V, which launched on PS3 and Xbox 360 in September 2013, but didn't arrive on PC until April 2015 – a substantial 19-month wait. Notably, the PS4 and Xbox One generations did not receive any new mainline GTA titles.

This historical data strongly suggests that GTA VI will likely adhere to Rockstar's established console-first strategy, leaving PC players to ponder how long they might have to wait after the initial console launch.

## Key Takeaways
- Only GTA 1 and GTA 2 were released on PC first or simultaneously with consoles.
- From GTA III onwards, all mainline GTA titles have debuted on consoles before their PC release.
- PC release delays for GTA games have ranged from approximately 7 months (GTA III, Vice City, IV) to 19 months (GTA V) after initial console launch.
- The trend indicates a growing gap between console and PC releases over time.
- The historical pattern suggests GTA VI will likely follow a console-first release, with PC players facing a potential waiting period.
