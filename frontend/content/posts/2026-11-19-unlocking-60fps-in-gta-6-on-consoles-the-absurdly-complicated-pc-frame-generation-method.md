# Unlocking 60fps in GTA 6 on Consoles: The Absurdly Complicated PC Frame Generation Method

Grand Theft Auto VI has launched on consoles, immediately impressing with its stunning visuals, though it's capped at 30 frames per second. For players yearning for the smoother 60fps experience, a remarkably intricate workaround has emerged, leveraging PC-based frame generation. This method utilizes an open-source real-time video enhancer called Veyra.

Achieving 60fps requires a significant hardware investment and a convoluted setup. Players need an HDMI 2.1-capable 4K capture card connected to a powerful PC via a certified USB-C 3.2 Gen 2 cable. The PC itself must house a robust GPU, ideally an Nvidia RTX 40-series or newer to utilize Tensor cores for frame generation. An OLED monitor is also highly recommended for its low response times, minimizing perceived latency.

Veyra captures console gameplay, processes it through the PC using various upscaling and frame generation technologies (from Nvidia, AMD, or Intel), and then displays the enhanced footage. While the system aims to minimize latency, some additional delay is unavoidable due to the complex signal chain. Early user feedback on platforms like Reddit suggests Veyra is effective at mitigating this.

Despite the technical marvel, the article questions the practicality and value of such an endeavor. Investing in this setup likely means acquiring a PC powerful enough to run GTA 6 natively at similar or better performance once the PC version eventually releases. The cost of a high-end capture card, specialized cables, and a potent PC makes this an 'overkill' solution for most. The author admires the dedication but warns of potential troubleshooting headaches over actual gameplay enjoyment. Ultimately, it's a niche solution for the most dedicated tech enthusiasts seeking a smoother experience at launch.
