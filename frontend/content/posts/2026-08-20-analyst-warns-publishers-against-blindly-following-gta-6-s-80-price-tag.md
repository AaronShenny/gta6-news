# Analyst Warns Publishers Against Blindly Following GTA 6's $80 Price Tag

The upcoming Grand Theft Auto 6 is set to retail its standard edition for $80, a price point that has sparked discussion across the gaming industry. Industry analyst Rhys Elliott from Ampere Analytics warns that other publishers might learn the 'wrong lessons' from this, potentially pushing their own 'run-of-the-mill or new-IP games' to $80.

Elliott argues that the correct approach for most publishers should be to maintain a $70 base price for their games. To cater to dedicated fans and generate additional revenue, he suggests layering premium editions on top, priced between $80 and $100. These premium versions would include incentives such as early access, cosmetic items, or season passes, allowing 'FOMO-prone superfans' to pay more while the broader audience retains a more accessible base price or waits for discounts.

This discussion comes amidst rising living costs and increasing expenses in the gaming sector, including console prices that could potentially exceed $1,000. While an $80 price for most AAA games is a tough sell, the popularity of GTA 6's $100 edition indicates a segment of players willing to pay a premium. Notably, Nintendo has largely maintained a $70 price point for its major releases, like Donkey Kong Bananza, demonstrating a more cautious approach to price hikes.

## Key Takeaways

- Rising living costs and increasing console prices make game pricing a critical factor for the average consumer.
