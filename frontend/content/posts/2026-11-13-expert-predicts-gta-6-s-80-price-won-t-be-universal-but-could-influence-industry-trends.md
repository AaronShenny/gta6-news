# Expert Predicts GTA 6's $80 Price Won't Be Universal, But Could Influence Industry Trends

Rockstar's highly anticipated *Grand Theft Auto 6* is set to release on November 19, carrying an $80 price tag. This marks a $20 increase from *GTA 5* (2013) and $10 more than many current AAA titles. Industry expert Rhys Elliott of Alinea Analytics suggests that while *GTA 6*'s success at this price point will undoubtedly encourage some publishers, $80 will **not** become the universal new normal for all games.

Elliott categorizes *GTA 6* as an "inelastic" entertainment product, meaning its immense demand allows it to command a higher price without significant sales impact. He cautions against the industry learning the "wrong lessons," as *GTA 6*'s unique appeal doesn't translate to every title. He warns that a player's $100 expenditure on *GTA 6* directly reduces their budget for other games, creating a zero-sum scenario for many.

Games that Elliott believes could similarly justify an $80 price include future installments in the *God of War* series, next-gen Naughty Dog titles, *Intergalactic: The Heretic Prophet*, upcoming *Forza* games (if delisted from Game Pass), new From Software releases, and *Hogwarts Legacy 2*. Conversely, he expects mid-tier AAA publishers and those offering smaller games or remakes to continue pricing downward, typically between $40-$60, citing examples like *Expedition 33* ($50), *Phantom Blade Zero* ($60), and *Oblivion Remastered* ($50).

Several factors complicate the pricing discussion. Adjusted for inflation, *GTA 6*'s $80 price is effectively cheaper than *GTA 5*'s $60 launch price in 2013 (which would be $86 today). However, this contrasts sharply with stagnant wages and rising costs for everyday goods like gas and groceries, making an $80 game a significant purchase for many consumers. Furthermore, console prices are at an all-time high, partly due to the AI craze.

The industry's historical reluctance to raise game prices has led to a proliferation of monetization strategies like DLC, battle passes, and microtransactions. Xbox, for instance, is exploring in-game advertising to offset ballooning development budgets rather than solely relying on price increases.
