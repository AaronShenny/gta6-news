# Rockstar Promises Shorter Waits for Future Games After GTA 6's Lengthy Development

Rockstar Games is committed to reducing the lengthy development cycles seen with `Grand Theft Auto 6`, which has taken over eight years to produce. According to Phil Hooker, Rockstar's head of development, the substantial investments made in the game's engine, tools, and team expansion will serve as a robust foundation for future titles, enabling quicker turnarounds.

Hooker emphasized that the studio intends to "go more sideways than upwards" by building upon existing technology rather than constantly rebuilding it from scratch. This strategic shift is aimed at preventing the significant gaps between major releases, such as the 2013 launch of `GTA 5`, the 2018 release of `Red Dead Redemption 2`, and the highly anticipated `GTA 6` in November 2026.

The company has undergone massive growth, nearly doubling its staff from 2,441 employees around `RDR2`'s release to 4,711 across 12 offices. Rockstar has a historical precedent for this approach, with `Grand Theft Auto: Vice City` following `GTA 3` in less than a year by leveraging existing tech. While no specific next game has been announced, and there's no guarantee for a rapid follow-up within the `Grand Theft Auto` series, the technical groundwork laid by `GTA 6` is expected to streamline future projects significantly. Currently, Rockstar's primary focus remains on the imminent launch of `GTA 6`, projected to be one of the biggest entertainment releases ever.
