# GTA 6 Cut This Feature Because It Was Too Real, But One Lifelike Detail Is Staying

Rockstar Games initially experimented with a grocery shopping and fridge restocking system for protagonists Jason and Lucia in GTA 6. However, this feature was ultimately cut because it was deemed "too realistic" and complicated the mechanics, not aligning with the studio's desired balance between "realism and ease of play." This cut system was presumably linked to the game's new body weight mechanics, where characters can gain weight based on eating habits and hit the gym to bulk up.

Despite removing the grocery feature, Rockstar is retaining a realistic car refueling and recharging system for both gas and electric vehicles. Players will need to strategically refuel their cars at gas stations, a process that is quick (around 10 seconds), inexpensive, and includes its own animation. Gas stations will also offer car repair services. Rockstar's Rob Nelson acknowledged that this refueling system might be divisive but confirmed the studio is committed to it, as it adds a layer of strategic thinking, particularly before missions like robberies.

Unlike GTA 5, where cars have unlimited fuel by default (unless the tank is punctured), GTA 6 will require active fuel management. GTA 6 is set to launch on November 19 for PlayStation 5 and Xbox Series X|S. The game is expected to be a massive financial success, with reports suggesting pre-orders alone could generate an estimated $2 billion, potentially covering its entire production budget. The base edition of GTA 6 will cost $80, while a $100 Ultimate Edition will offer exclusive in-game content.
