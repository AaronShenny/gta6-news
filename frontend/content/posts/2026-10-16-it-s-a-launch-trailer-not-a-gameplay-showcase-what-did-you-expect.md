# It’s a launch trailer not a gameplay showcase What did you expect?

The article, originally a Reddit post, addresses the community's strong reactions to the visual effects, such as bloom and motion blur, seen in the recent Grand Theft Auto 6 launch trailer. The author argues that it is illogical to expect a "cinematic launch trailer" to represent raw gameplay footage. Such trailers are intentionally designed with cinematic camera angles, lighting, and visual effects to create a movie-like experience, which is distinct from actual gameplay. The post reminds readers that Rockstar Games typically releases separate gameplay trailers, and suggests that another one might be forthcoming. Drawing parallels to Red Dead Redemption 2's marketing, where initial doubts about screenshots were later dispelled by impressive gameplay footage, the author urges patience. The core message is that judging the entirety of GTA 6's visual quality based solely on a cinematic trailer is premature and that Rockstar, given its reputation for highly detailed and visually stunning games, deserves the benefit of the doubt until more definitive gameplay footage is released.

## Key Takeaways
- The recent GTA 6 trailer is a 'launch trailer,' not a 'gameplay showcase,' designed for cinematic impact.
- Cinematic trailers intentionally use effects like bloom, motion blur, and specific camera angles to create a movie-like experience.
- These cinematic effects do not necessarily reflect the final in-game graphics or suggest excessive blur in actual gameplay.
- Rockstar's marketing often includes separate, more detailed gameplay trailers after initial cinematic reveals.
- Past Rockstar titles, like RDR2, also faced initial visual scrutiny that was later disproven by actual gameplay footage.
- It is premature to judge GTA 6's overall visual quality based solely on a cinematic trailer.
- Rockstar deserves the benefit of the doubt until more raw gameplay footage is officially released.
