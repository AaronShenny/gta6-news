# GTA 6 Unveils Groundbreaking Dynamic Weather System, Featuring Hurricanes and Localized Storms

Rockstar Games is set to revolutionize environmental realism in Grand Theft Auto 6 with a "re-engineered" weather system, as confirmed by Owen Shepherd, the studio's vice president of art for lighting and rendering, in an interview with Game Informer. The goal for GTA 6's setting, Leonida (a fictionalized Florida), is to achieve an unprecedented "dynamically solid" and scaled weather experience.

The new system boasts "fully dynamic cloudscapes that roll across the map," offering a vast array of weather conditions. Reflecting Florida's unique climate, the game will feature highly localized rain showers, where one direction might show sunshine while another experiences a downpour, complete with emerging rainbows. Post-rain effects like persistent wet surfaces and puddles will also be present.

A sophisticated new wind system is also being implemented, accounting for "offshore breezes after sunset, convectional updrafts, and occlusion from buildings." This will manifest visually through swirling hair and debris on rooftops, ripples across water surfaces, and bending grass in swampy areas. Crucially, GTA 6 will include tropical storms and hurricanes, alongside thunder and lightning, though the potential gameplay impact of these extreme weather events, such as structural damage, remains unconfirmed.

## Key Takeaways
- GTA 6 features a 're-engineered' weather system designed for unprecedented realism and scale in its Leonida (Florida-inspired) setting.
- The system includes fully dynamic cloudscapes, offering a broad spectrum of weather across the map.
- Localized rain showers, rainbows, and persistent wet surfaces with puddles will accurately replicate Florida's climate.
- A new wind system will dynamically affect environmental elements, including hair, debris, water, and grass.
- Tropical storms, hurricanes, thunder, and lightning are confirmed to be part of the game's dynamic weather.
- The gameplay impact of extreme weather events, such as potential structural damage, has not yet been detailed.
