# Take-Two CEO Compares GTA 6 Marketing to 'Barbie' Movie Strategy

Take-Two Interactive CEO Strauss Zelnick has elaborated on the company's aggressive marketing strategy for the highly anticipated Grand Theft Auto 6, drawing a surprising parallel to 2023's The Barbie Movie. Zelnick emphasized that even for a globally recognized and beloved franchise like GTA, extensive marketing is crucial to convert mere awareness into genuine enthusiasm. He noted that The Barbie Movie, despite its well-known IP, executed a 'phenomenal marketing campaign' that was 'everywhere' and ultimately 'worked'.Zelnick explained that for any 'broad-based entertainment' property, companies have no choice but to invest significantly in marketing new releases. He addressed investor questions regarding the necessity of a large marketing push for a game 'everyone on Earth knows about,' stating that his experience as a former movie executive taught him that while a bad product can't be saved by marketing, good marketing can significantly amplify excitement for a quality product. The goal, he asserted, is always to 'take awareness and turn it into excitement.'Furthermore, Zelnick touched upon Rockstar's collaboration with Netflix for an extended GTA 6 trailer, describing it as a 'tutorial' designed to show fans the game in action and highlight its appeal. He praised Rockstar for finding an entertaining way to convey gameplay information, differing from traditional in-game tutorials. Grand Theft Auto 6 is slated for a November 19 release on PS5 and Xbox Series X|S, with a PC version expected to follow. In related news, 'GTA 6: The Album' has also been announced, notably on physical media.

## Key Takeaways
- Take-Two CEO Strauss Zelnick believes aggressive marketing is essential for GTA 6, despite its immense hype.
- He compares GTA 6's marketing approach to 'The Barbie Movie,' which successfully generated widespread enthusiasm for a known IP.
- The goal of marketing is to transform global awareness into genuine excitement and show players 'what they’re going to get'.
- Zelnick learned from his movie executive days that good marketing amplifies excitement for a quality product, while bad marketing can deter.
- The extended GTA 6 trailer released via Netflix served as an innovative 'tutorial' to explain gameplay and appeal to potential buyers.
